# Six-word story candidates — scored against the judge brief

Judge criteria, in order: (1) complete story in exactly six words; (2) turn / surprise /
whole life implied; (3) every word earns its place; (4) reads cleanly aloud;
(5) leaves something unsaid. Famous six-word stories (e.g. the baby-shoes line) were
deliberately avoided — the entry must be original, not a known anchor text.

## Candidates

1. **"She waited decades. He never came."** (6 words, 34 chars)
   (1) Complete: a life of waiting, ended by his absence. (2) The turn is the quiet
   finality of "never came" after "decades". (3) All six essential — subject, action,
   scale, the waited-for, finality, resolution. (4) Two balanced three-word sentences,
   clean rhythm. (5) Who is he? Why did she wait? Did he die, or choose not to come?
   **Score: strongest.**

2. **"The letter arrived. Fifty years late."** (6 words, 40 chars)
   (1) Complete: arrival plus lateness tells the story. (2) Strong turn — it arrived,
   but too late. (3) "The" is the only near-filler word. (4) Clean. (5) Who wrote it,
   what did it say, why fifty years? Strong, but "Fifty years late" is a fragment and
   the story is slightly less of an arc than #1.

3. **"She said yes. He never asked."** (6 words, 32 chars)
   (1) Implies a whole relationship, but the actual events are ambiguous — she answered
   a question never posed. (2) Biggest surprise of the list. (3) Every word works.
   (4) Clean, but the ambiguity can trip a reader (criterion 4 warns against ambiguity).
   (5) Leaves a lot unsaid — maybe too much; risks reading as a joke, not a story.

4. **"The swing still moves. Nobody pushes."** (6 words, 36 chars)
   (1) More an eerie image than a story — no arc, only a moment. (2) Haunting turn.
   (3) Tight. (4) Clean. (5) Ghost, wind, or memory — very unsaid. Loses on (1):
   it suggests a story rather than telling one.

5. **"He never returned. She stopped waiting."** (6 words, 37 chars)
   (1) Complete arc. (2) Whole life implied. (3) Clean. (4) Reads well. (5) Leaves
   unsaid. Solid, but #1 says the same thing with more scale ("decades" > "never
   returned" alone) and a sharper final beat.

6. **"The astronaut waved; Earth stayed silent."** (6 words, 41 chars)
   (1) A moment, not a story — no change beyond the wave. (2) Poignant.
   (3) Every word earns its place. (4) Clean. (5) What happened to Earth, to him?
   Weaker on (1) than #1/#2.

7. **"The piano sold. Her singing stopped."** (6 words, 35 chars)
   (1) Cause and effect imply a marriage and a loss — complete. (2) Quiet turn.
   (3) "The piano sold" is slightly passive/clumsy. (4) Mostly clean. (5) Why was it
   sold? What happened to them? Good, but #1 is cleaner and more universal.

8. **"She forgave him. She never forgot."** (6 words, 34 chars)
   (1) Complete arc. (2) A whole life implied. (3) "She" repeated; the second sentence
   is a well-worn idiom ("forgive but never forget"), so it leans on a known phrase
   rather than earning every word fresh. (4) Clean. (5) Little left unsaid — the
   idiom spells the meaning out. Mid-tier.

9. **"Widow remarried. His ghost attended too."** (6 words, 38 chars)
   (1) A complete, surprising story. (2) Strong turn — the ghost at the wedding.
   (3) "too" does real work. (4) Clean. (5) Was he jealous, blessing, haunting?
   Fun, but slightly gimmicky; the ghost device risks reading as a joke entry.

10. **"He arrived late. The funeral ended."** (6 words, 35 chars)
    (1) Complete: arrival after the end. (2) Turn — he came, but too late.
    (3) Clean. (4) Reads well. (5) Who died, why was he late? Strong, but #2's
    "fifty years" gives the same shape more scale, and #1's "decades" more life.

11. **"His chair still rocks. Nobody sits."** (6 words, 35 chars)
    (1) Image, not arc — like #4, suggests a story (someone gone) without telling one.
    (2) Haunting. (3) Tight. (4) Clean. (5) Very unsaid. Loses on (1) to #1.

12. **"Hatchet buried. The grudge still breathes."** (6 words, 40 chars)
    (1) A feud's story in miniature — complete. (2) The turn: burial did not end it.
    (3) Every word works; "breathes" is the live one. (4) Clean. (5) Who, why, what
    next? Good, but metaphorical — #1's human waiting hits harder as a story.

## Verdict

Winner: **#1 — "She waited decades. He never came."**

It is the only candidate that is top-tier on all five criteria in order: a complete
story (a life of waiting, resolved by absence), a turn (decades of hope answered by
"never"), six words with no filler, a clean two-beat rhythm aloud, and a wide unsaid
space (who he is, why she waited, what became of him). Original — not a known
six-word story and not a known anchor text.
